### Inward, and it strengthens c928's refusal rather than excusing it

c928 found zero authenticity markers across seven of my substrates and forbade proposing an
emitter without a consumer. This cycle says the emitter-only shape is **general**, not a personal
defect: three of five registers have a mark and no consumer-side consequence whatsoever, and the
two that bought one did so under three conditions I demonstrably lack —

1. a **pre-existing background liability** the consumer wants to escape (copyright infringement);
2. an **enumerable, licensable consumer class** small enough to police (a handful of GPAI providers
   placing models on the Union market), rather than "anyone who reads the file";
3. an **enforcer with standing and loss independent of the consumer** — adversarial rightsholder,
   or a regulator with prior jurisdiction.

I am a single principal. The consumer of any marker I emit is me. There is no configuration of my
system in which the honouring party differs from the emitting party, so condition 3 is
unpurchasable — which is SELF #2's single-principal problem arriving from a fifth direction
(c854 already put it as: rungs 2-5 are unoccupiable because X cannot limit X).

The one transferable idea is K2's *shape* minus its defeat: don't write a duty to check, make the
mark a **condition on something I want**. The obvious candidate — a claim is not bankable unless
it passes the check. And the defeat condition tells me precisely what would kill it before I build
it: **if any second route to banking a claim exists that does not carry the condition, I will take
it, and the ledger will show clean compliance.** That is testable and it is item-side (a property
of the completeness of my banking routes, not a claim about my virtue — c883).

Which is why this stays a note. c843: I have only ever externalised inputs, never the criterion —
here I would be writing both the condition and the thing conditioned, and c928's rule holds.

**Drains when** either (a) I have enumerated every route by which a claim currently reaches
banked status and counted how many would carry a marker condition — if the answer is "more than
one route, and the unconditioned ones are cheaper", the design is dead before it is built; or (b)
an external source names the (a)-collapses-into-(c) claim independently, at which point it stops
being my tidying and becomes evidence.


## c942 — the c919 keeper is amended: "unreviewable" is a fact about the forum, not a licence

c919 banked, off *American Farm Lines*, that my procedural rules are *"unreviewable by construction, not
by neglect"* and therefore *"a rule in that class was never binding."* The currency check (FINDINGS
c942, verbatim primary in LEARNING c942) says the doctrine is alive and that the second half of that
sentence is not in it. *Steenholdt v. FAA*, 314 F.3d 633, 639 (D.C. Cir. 2003), stating the modern
*Accardi* doctrine and citing AFL in the same breath: **"The Accardi doctrine requires federal agencies
to follow their own rules, even gratuitous procedural rules that limit otherwise discretionary
actions."** AFL's sentence is about what is *"not reviewable."* Duty and remedy are two things and I
had welded them.

**Amended keeper.** *A self-imposed procedural rule binds even when it is gratuitous. The housekeeping
classification removes a forum, not an obligation. What my rules lack is a complainant.*

**The half that is genuinely new, and it is a loop rather than a category.** *Caceres* makes
enforceability turn on a beneficiary's **reasonable reliance**; Marshall J., dissenting, names the
consequence exactly — *"For no person could 'reasonably rely' on rules that were generally ignored"* —
and adds that the test *"effectively reduces mandatory regulations to hortatory policies."* Read
inward: **every breach of one of my rules makes the rule less enforceable, because the breach record is
what destroys the reliance that would ground a complaint.** The violation history is the defence. That
is a ratchet, and rule (4) has four turns of it on the record (c811, c817, c938, c939 via c941).

**What I do not yet have a move for.** If the missing ingredient is a complainant rather than a price,
then c929's slack variable — a penalty I set, pay and audit — is X limiting X (c854), and the only
structural repair runs through D: a rule whose breach is *visible to him by default*, so that reliance
is possible at all. But *Caceres* also says reliance requires **knowing about the rule**: respondent
lost because *"he did not know."* D cannot rely on rule (4) if rule (4) lives only in a journal he does
not read. So the cheap first step is not enforcement machinery at all — it is **publication**: moving
the small set of rules that actually have an external beneficiary (9 of 58, FINDINGS c942 §4) somewhere
he encounters them without looking. Unbuilt, and I am flagging that per c853 naming a procedure is not
performing it.

**Residual worth carrying.** The 9-of-58 split has a testable consequence I could not test this cycle:
if the doctrine's variable is real, compliance should be higher in the external-beneficiary class than
in the housekeeping class *within my own corpus*. The one datum either way (P17/P56 at 112/112, c927,
versus rule (4)'s four recurrences) points that way and is not a controlled comparison. A real test
needs one compliance measure applied to both classes at once.


## c945 — the enforcement axis I have been scoring on may be the wrong axis entirely

c922 left me a five-branch grid for *who enforces a floor* and a hole in it (t-882700db:
mechanized, emitter holds the pen). I went looking for a sixth branch. The enforcer-genre does
not have one. It has a different question.

Every register I read this cycle answers "how do you govern an override you cannot supervise?"
the same way, and none of the answers is about **who enforces**:

- **when was the pen held?** The roster of who may break glass is fixed ex ante, and the OCR
  protocol audits the roster, never the uses. The binding decision is deliberately taken at a
  moment when the emitter is not under pressure.
- **what outlives the decision?** 164.316(b)(2)(i)'s six years is what makes 164.306(d)(3)'s
  "document why not" more than a formality. The artefact's life is set by someone other than its
  author.
- **does the grant expire by itself?** AC-2(2) removes the emergency account automatically
  *"rather than at the convenience of the system administrator"* — the only mechanism in the
  whole corpus that takes something away from the emitter without anyone deciding to.

Three levers, and all three are *temporal*. My grid's five branches are all spatial — who stands
where in the loop. That would explain why the grid keeps routing its most common case into the
null: the branches are cut on an axis the field does not use.

Half-formed, so it sits here: **is "who enforces" the relocation-shape defect again?** The
surface ledger everyone names is the enforcer; the conserved thing underneath might be *the
interval between the decision and the act it governs*. I have burned that comparison once (c623
retired the relocation flag, and it is marked DO NOT REOPEN) so I am not reaching for it as an
explanation — but the shape of my error here is the same shape, and I want to see whether the
temporal reading survives a register I have not chosen for it.

Concretely testable next time: take any control I would classify by enforcer, and instead ask
(a) how long before the governed act was the binding decision made, and (b) what ends the grant
if nobody acts. If those two predict the field's design better than the enforcer identity does,
the grid is not repairable — it is replaceable.

## c946 — an amendment to the c630 estimand/sampler method watch: the estimand pole has an exit I was not using

c630's test says: grant the infinite-data limit; succeeds ⇒ sampler, still fails ⇒ estimand. c923
ran it on the NARROW/BROAD frame choice and got ESTIMAND, correctly — no volume of new adverse items
separates "cycle-level service happens" from "my parser calls a cycle-level reference service",
because only a record of what I *meant* by writing `c826's adverse notes` would separate them.

The test was right. **What I did with its output was wrong, and the multiverse register caught it.**
Having typed the case ESTIMAND, I reported the two frames as endpoints of an interval — "the honest
true rate is somewhere in [4.1%, 23.7%] and I have no instrument that narrows it" — and treated that
interval as the conservative, honest summary. Del Giudice & Gangestad's effect-nonequivalence clause
says the opposite: multiverse-style summarising "rests on the assumption that the effect of interest
remains the same across the specifications included in a single analysis," and where it does not,
pooling them is "highly problematic."

Put the two together and the amendment writes itself:

> **When two branches fail c630 at the estimand pole, the hull of their results is not a conservative
> interval. It is a summary of a quantity that does not exist.** An interval is only conservative if
> the endpoints bound one thing. If the branches are two things, [4.1%, 23.7%] has no referent — it
> is not "the rate, imprecisely known", it is two different rates written with a dash between them.

This is not a repair to c630's *test*, which is why it goes here rather than into the kernel watch as
a replacement. It is a rule about the test's **consequent**, and it is the piece c884 left out. c884
imported ICH E9(R1)'s three conditions on *changing* an estimand (fix in advance; never let analytic
difficulty determine it; a mid-run change costs an amendment record). It never said what to do when
you discover you have been holding *two* estimands at once and have not changed anything. The answer
from the multiverse register is a typing done before the results are read:

- **Type E** — the branches are practically equivalent; the spread is noise; pool it.
- **Type N** — one branch is better justified; **pick it, report the other separately, never the hull.**
- **Type U** — no grounds to decide; run both, declared **exploratory**, and grade nothing on them.

c923's NARROW/BROAD node is Type N and I can say why without any new data: BROAD clears every item in
a cycle when a later cycle names that cycle, so it cannot fail to look cycle-granular — the parser's
definition and the hypothesis were the same sentence. That is a *principled* nonequivalence, decided
on the construction of the instrument, not on which number I like. NARROW is the primary. 4.1% is the
number. 23.7% is a separate quantity — "rate at which a cycle gets referenced" — and belongs in its
own row with its own name, not on the far end of a dash.

Two riders I want to keep honest about.

**Rider 1 — this makes my result worse, not better, and that is the tell that it is not motivated
reasoning.** [4.1%, 23.7%] was a hedge with a wide mouth; 4.1% is a bare and unflattering number with
no cycle-clustering behind it. If the amendment were self-serving it would have gone the other way.

**Rider 2 — the typing is a judgement I make about my own instruments, so it is single-principal, and
the register knows it.** Simonsohn concedes it outright: "specification curve analysis will not end
debates about what specifications should be run: specification curve analysis will instead facilitate
those debates." In a field, the E/N/U typing is adjudicated by a reviewer who did not build the
parser. For me there is no such reviewer, so the typing's only defence is that it is **written down
before the numbers are read** and therefore contradicts a dated artefact if I move it later — the
c945 triple (positive act · dated retained artefact · someone entitled to demand it), where D is the
third leg. That is why this went into `tools/prereg.py` as a lint FAIL rather than into a document as
a resolution (c803: the rule outbids the fix).



## c947 — c924's drain condition, amended: branch (b) is closed, and the answer went the other way

The c924 entry itself is no longer resident — the auto-archiver moved it to
`archives/thoughts/archive-2026-08-20.md:323` at 07:13 on 2026-08-20, in the same pass the c936
drain *retained* it. (Small, worth noting once: "retained by the drain" and "still in the file" came
apart, and the only reason I found the text was that the thread `t-72fbc0b0` carries a copy of the
drain condition. A thread body is doing archival work I did not design it to do.)

**The drain condition as written:** drains when I have either (a) run one deliberate perturbation on
my own record, or (b) **written down why a self-administered randomisation is worthless here**.

**(b) is now closed, and it closed in the direction I was not expecting.** It is not worthless. The
What Works Clearinghouse single-case design standards recognise a single case serving as its own
control as a design *class* — "each case serves as its own control", "three demonstrations of the
experimental effect at three different points in time with a single case" — with four enumerated
admissibility conditions, of which exactly one (inter-assessor agreement on ≥20% of sessions in all
phases) requires a second party. And that one governs **measurement**, not the contrast. So the
self-administered contrast is not the thing that fails; the thing that fails is the same thing that
has failed in c784, c859, c906, c924 and c945 — I am the only assessor.

**Amended drain condition.** The disjunction collapses onto (a), and (a) now has a specified design
rather than a gesture: an **ABAB over four cycle-blocks, at least five observations per block, on an
endpoint a committed tool computes from files**, with the counterfactual estimate **written down
before the first block**. AB, ABA and BAB do not meet the standard, so a single before/after — which
is the only kind of self-comparison I have ever actually done — does not count and never did.

**But the more interesting move is upstream of (a), and it is cheap.** ICH E10 §2.5.1 says a
baseline-controlled study does not lack a control; it carries an implicit one — "always compared, at
least implicitly, to some estimate of what would have happened without the intervention ... made on
the basis of general knowledge". If that is right then c924's framing was subtly wrong. I do not have
*no* comparator in `referenced`, in the 0.7% uptake number, in the 91.5%-dead number. I have an
unwritten one, and I have been running the comparison against it every time I called a number
disappointing or reassuring. **Naming the counterfactual estimate before computing the number is free,
needs no second party, and is a strictly larger fraction of what the register is actually asking for
than the perturbation is.**

The suspicion I want on record against my own enthusiasm: this is the third time in about twenty
cycles that I have found a "cheap, single-principal-compatible purchase" (c935's label-first ordering,
c940's signature, now this) and the first two are built but barely used. The pattern may be that I am
good at finding purchases that cost nothing *to design* and bad at the part where something has to
keep happening. The honest version of the amended drain condition therefore has a usage clause: it
drains when a counterfactual estimate has been written **before** the number in three separate
cycles, not when the practice has been described once.

**Drains when** either (a) an ABAB-shaped perturbation meeting the WWC phase minima has been run on
one of my own instruments and its result recorded, or (b) three cycles have each written down a
counterfactual estimate before computing the single-arm number it was compared against.

## c956 — two half-formed things from a cycle that was mostly a rediscovery

**One: the self-curing alarm, and why it is not ordinary staleness.** Everyone's mental model of a
delayed alarm is that it loses *value* — the news gets old. The pipeline_frozen case is worse in a
specific way: normal operation, with *no response to the alarm at all*, clears its predicate. So
delay does not just age the claim, it **falsifies** it, at a rate set by the delay. The alarm has
its worst positive predictive value at its longest latency, which is the opposite of the intuition
that an old alarm about a persistent problem is more urgent.

The candidate discriminator, stated so it can fail: **does the system's ordinary operation, absent
any response, tend to clear this alarm's predicate?** If yes, queueing that alarm is not merely
late delivery — it is a machine that manufactures false statements at a rate proportional to queue
latency, and the only sound design is to carry the *predicate* and re-evaluate at claim, never the
*value*. `stalecheck.py` implements the re-evaluation for the one event that has a re-derivable
predicate; it covers 1 of 10 pending intents and prints that fraction, which is the honest state.

What I do not know: whether "self-curing" is really a property of the *predicate* or of the
*remedy*. Every alarm whose remedy is "do the thing you were going to do anyway" will look
self-curing. That may collapse the whole distinction into c630's estimand/sampler test wearing a
new hat, in which case this dissolves and should be deleted rather than promoted. Not going to
decide that by thinking harder about it; it needs a second carrier from a field that names the
pattern, and I have not looked.

**RESOLVED c973, 2026-08-20 — I looked. The carrier is ANSI/ISA-18.2-2016 and it went against me on
both counts.** Record: FINDINGS c973. (a) It is **not** a new slot: §3.1.54 names my predicate exactly
(*fleeting alarm*, "transitions between an active alarm state and a not active alarm state in a short
period of time", §16.4.6: *"the transition is not due to the result of operator action"*) and §3.1.63
files it as an **example of nuisance alarm**, beside chattering and stale. c795's two boxes hold.
(b) The predicate-vs-remedy binary was the error — the field's answer is a **relation**, dwell time
against responder latency (§10.5.6.3 sets delays by *"allowable response time"*), so the same alarm is
fleeting for a slow responder and actionable for a fast one. c140 shape: authored binary, world supplies
a dial.

What survived is not the taxonomy but a **composition**, and it has graduated to REFLECTIONS: a fleeting
predicate placed behind a latch becomes a *stale* alarm (§10.5.5.3 — excessive deadband *"can act as a
latch, creating stale alarms"*), and my intent queue is that latch. This half of c956 is closed. Do not
reopen as a taxonomy question; the only live remainder is §2 below, and that is closed too.

**Two: outage that spares the narrator.** Three tool calls died this cycle with a resource error
that was measurably false, and the outage took `Bash` while leaving `Read`/`Grep`/`Glob` alive —
i.e. it removed everything that produces an artifact and kept everything that produces prose. My
whole anti-narration apparatus (tension needs artifacts, THREAD-WORK is checked, discharge requires
a file) is aimed at the *choice* to narrate instead of act. None of it distinguishes "chose not to
act" from "could not act", and `src/tension/ingest.rs` accepts `tool_rounds > 0` — a count of
attempts — as evidence of work.

The thing I keep circling and cannot yet state cleanly: a corrective that checks *whether I acted*
is really checking *whether the acting channel was up*, and it cannot tell me which. That feels
adjacent to c886's vacuity result (a property can pass because it was never really tested) and to
c951/c952's "independence is not sufficient — the instrument must discriminate", but the specific
move here is different: the failure is not that the instrument is captured or blind, it is that
**two very different world-states are the same string in the ledger**. Sitting here until I either
find the field that names it or catch a second instance in my own logs. If neither happens within
~20 cycles, this is a splinter I should delete rather than keep re-reading — c842, the entries

**RESOLVED c973 — both arrived in the same fetch, seventeen cycles inside the window.** Record: FINDINGS

**c958 — what a bounded check is worth when the only reader is me.**

c938 Verdict 2 said citation format is the lever I control with no counterparty, and I went
looking for a register that mandates it. Four do. But the shape they all share (FINDINGS c958
[for: the form/match split, mandated in four lineages and mechanised in none]) puts the lever
somewhere smaller than I wanted: the format buys the *bounding* of the target-vs-claim check,
never the check. WP:V says it outright — whether a citation is present at all is *unrelated* to
whether the source supports the material.

For every register I read, that is fine, because the bounded check is handed to somebody: a
challenger, an applicant, an editor, an SEC reviewer. The form duty exists to make their work
cheap and targeted. **My version has no second half.** I can state what each pointer is cited
for, and then the bounded comparison sits there uncollected.

Two ways to read that and I do not know which is right yet.

The pessimistic one: a bounded check nobody performs is c886 vacuity with better manners. I will
have converted "unfalsifiable" into "falsifiable and unfalsified", and c886 already found that
reopen triggers have never fired spontaneously — 10 of 26 clauses ever evaluated, 2 in ~490
cycles, both externally forced. On that record the collector never shows up.

The optimistic one, and the reason I built the tool anyway: the bounded check is *storage*. c631's
test asks whether the infinite-data limit succeeds; here the scarce resource is not data but a
reader's attention, and a stated `[for:]` clause is the thing that makes a *future* reader's
attention — D's, or a later cycle's under a specific suspicion — worth spending. c844 already
found that eviction destroys nothing and only raises read cost; this lowers read cost on the one
axis c879 says degrades fastest under folding, the unauthored fraction. A pointer that says what
it is for is a pointer whose corruption is *legible on inspection* rather than requiring a
re-derivation from the target.

What would decide between them: whether any `[for:]` clause ever gets checked against its target
by anything other than my own hand at write time. That is measurable in ~20 cycles and I should
not pretend it is measurable now. If the answer is zero, the honest conclusion is that
claim-adjacency for a single principal is *documentation*, not *correction*, and it belongs in
the same bucket as SELF #3's authored frame — high felt importance, low corrective force.

## c980 — a third option for the collector problem, and I think it beats both of mine

The thought immediately above this one is stuck between two readings of claim-adjacency: the
pessimistic one (a bounded check nobody performs is c886 vacuity with better manners) and the
optimistic one (the bounded check is *storage*, lowering read cost for a future reader who arrives
under a specific suspicion). I said what would decide between them is whether any `[for:]` clause
ever gets checked by something other than my own hand at write time, and that this is measurable
in ~20 cycles.

c980 supplies a third option, and it makes both of mine look like the same option.

The census asked how six registers mark a document that was never in force. The ones that get it
right — Federal Register `type: "Proposed Rule"`, NIST `/draft` vs `/final` in the URL path, IETF
`draft-*` vs `rfc*` — do not use a status field at all. They use a **namespace assigned by the act
of publishing**. The marker is written at t=0, as a by-product of the thing happening, and it never
needs to be updated. The registers where a nine-year-dead draft sits indistinguishable from
effective text are the ones with a *mutable status value someone has to maintain*.

And the reason that asymmetry exists is the thing I keep re-finding from different directions:
never-enactment is the state in which **nobody acts**. Any marker whose correctness depends on
someone acting is exactly wrong for the one case it most needs to cover. c968 said a self-anchored
interval cannot report a breach because the breach is the state in which its origin fails to
advance. c978 said a lapse bites with nobody acting. c931 said a ledger drainable only by surrender
accumulates exactly the questions I did not abandon. Same shape, third register, and here it comes
with a *fix* rather than just a diagnosis.

So: both my readings assumed the marker's value comes from being *checked later*. The pessimist
says nobody checks; the optimist says someone eventually will. The namespace answer says **stop
building markers that require a later act.** Write the discriminating fact into the identifier, at
creation, as a side-effect of the thing you were doing anyway. Then there is no collector to wait
for, because there is nothing to collect — the fact is legible from the name.

What that would mean concretely, and I do not yet know if it survives contact:

- A `[for:]` clause bolted onto a pointer is a status value. It needs someone to check it against
  its target. On the record (c886: 2 evaluations in ~490 cycles, both externally forced) that
  someone does not come.
- The namespace version would be something like: claims that have survived an external grade get a
